This paper investigates the impact of floating-point non-associativity (FPNA) on reproducibility in high-performance computing (HPC) and deep learning (DL) applications, particularly in iterative algorithms and GPU-based deep learning models. FPNA introduces run-by-run variability in results, affecting the efficiency and reliability of HPC and DL applications.

The research specifically highlights the extreme sensitivity of DL models to FPNA, which complicates debugging and correctness testing, and examines strategies to mitigate these effects for reproducibility and correctness in scientific computing and industrial DL applications.
